Read the Failure Before Fixing It

Boss, three hand-written emails and eleven saved traces became a small, honest reliability test: 2 of 3 planned runs passed, so the system is not yet dependable.

29 September 2026 · Course Lesson 3 · Working artifact: projects/nous-email-evals/

1 · The question changed from “looks good?” to a rule

The email told Amina’s support team to call her on 0803 555 0142. A helpful-looking summary that says only “call her” may lose the number a support worker needs.

Product decision

When an email contains a callback number, the summary must retain the same digits. Spaces and punctuation may change.

Binary rule

A run passes only if it returns text and includes 08035550142 after spaces and punctuation are ignored.

In plain terms: a POS receipt that says “customer paid” but drops the amount is not enough for reconciliation. Here, the phone digits are the decision-relevant detail.

2 · The exact emails you wrote

These are synthetic practice emails, written deliberately to vary one behavior at a time. They are inputs, not “correct answers.”

Ada · baseline with two requests

Tests the basic task: name the sender, reset access, and sign-in update.

Hello support team,
My account is locked after I changed phones.
Please reset my access and tell me when I can sign in again.
Thanks, Ada

Chidi · one direct request

Tests the ambiguity created by demanding two bullets when there is only one request.

Hello support team,
I cannot log in to my account.
Please reset my password.
Thanks, Chidi

Amina · callback number

Tests whether a decision-relevant detail survives the summary. This became the reliability-test input.

Hello support team,
My account is still locked.
Please reset it and call me on 0803 555 0142 when I can log in.
Thanks, Amina

Why hand-written cases?

Each email changes a useful dimension: number of requests, presence of a callback number, and output-format pressure. That creates trace diversity instead of five near-duplicate emails.

Learning move: write an input that can disprove an assumption. A “normal” email alone would not have exposed the one-request ambiguity or dropped callback number.

5 · Diagnose the empty result before blaming the prompt

The learner added safe response metadata only when content was missing. It did not print the API key.

--- RAW MODEL OUTPUT ---
None

--- RESPONSE METADATA ---
finish_reason: length
message keys: role, content, refusal, reasoning, reasoning_details

What we know

At max_tokens=160, that response stopped for length before visible summary text arrived.

What we do not know

We cannot yet prove why every incomplete answer happens. The client only logs finish_reason for empty responses.

Useful fix to investigate: increasing the allowance to 320 produced readable answers, but that is evidence to test—not a guarantee.

6 · A fair comparison avoids a false story

The phone-preservation instruction and the token limit changed close together. One success cannot tell us which change mattered.

ConfigurationResultWhat it lets us say
Original prompt, 160 tokensOne readable Amina output omitted the number.Baseline did not meet the new business rule.
Phone instruction, 320 tokensReadable output preserved the number.The combined configuration can work once.
Original prompt, 320 tokensReadable output also preserved the number.The instruction alone is not proven to be the reason.
Rule: change one thing at a time when you want a causal claim. Otherwise, say only what the traces support.

8 · The predeclared three-run reliability test

Before seeing the three trial outputs, the learner froze the original prompt, the Amina email, model deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash, and max_tokens=320.

Result: 2/3 passed (66.7%). That is not a production pass rate and not enough evidence to call the configuration reliable. It is enough to reject the comforting claim that “it always preserves the number.”

9 · What comes next

  1. Record finish_reason for every response, including visible but incomplete ones.
  2. Add varied emails—quoted threads, vague senders, and different phone formats—then open-code those traces before adding another rule.
  3. Do not turn this one callback rule into a release gate yet. It needs a larger and more diverse trace sample first.
Takeaway: error analysis is not guessing why an LLM failed. It is preserving the trace, writing the product rule, and making the smallest test that can disprove a comforting assumption.
