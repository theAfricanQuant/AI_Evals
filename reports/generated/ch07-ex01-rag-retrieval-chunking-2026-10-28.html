Issue 07 RAG retrieval and chunking Trace · Test · Teach
Lead story · A chunk-boundary test

Four words of overlap brought the full answer into view

A tiny synthetic RAG lab found the correct evidence at rank one with overlap, while a no-overlap setting missed it even in the top two.

The question was: “How many days is the standard validity period?” We scored word overlap between the question and retrieved passages, then checked whether the full evidence sentence appeared in the retrieved context. The exercise used fictional mobile-support text and whitespace word counts, not a production retriever. Chapter 7 explains why retrieval and generation need separate checks.

With 16-word chunks and no overlap, the first passage ended at “validity period”; the second result was a related but different passage about travel passes. With 16-word chunks and 4-word overlap, the first result contained the complete sentence. A 24-word chunk also kept the evidence together, with more surrounding text. The chunking script records the comparison.

The idea in plain language

Overlap repeats a little context at the boundary

A chunk is a passage the retriever can return. The answer sentence crossed the 16-word cut. Repeating four words in the next chunk kept the sentence intact in one retrieved passage; a larger chunk also contained it.

Case file · Retrieval baseline

One hard query exposed a ranking miss

  1. Three labeled questions returned Recall@1 of 2/3 = 0.67.
  2. All three gold documents appeared in the top two: Recall@2 = 1.00.
  3. On the hard query, the billing-date distractor ranked first and the gold passage ranked second.
Decision rule: pass when the labeled evidence is in the top result. The baseline passed two of three queries.

The full results include Precision@2 = 0.50 and MRR = 0.83. See the labeled questions and study note.

Briefs · Retrieval and generation

Coverage, ranking, and grounding

Recall can hide ordering

Recall@2 reached 1.00 because every labeled passage appeared in the first two. Precision@2 was 0.50 because each pair also contained an irrelevant passage. Q3’s gold document at rank two lowered MRR. The baseline code prints all three measures.

The question was intentionally difficult

The hard query shared billing-cycle words with DOC-D, which did not answer the carryover question. DOC-A contained the answer but ranked second. See the synthetic corpus.

Faithfulness is a separate check

A hand-written “seven days” answer addressed the question but misapplied a travel-pass fact to the standard bundle. The “thirty days” answer matched the overlapping context. These are practice fixtures, not model outputs. See the labels.

Research note

Chapter 7 recommends measuring retrieval and generation separately and treating chunk size and overlap as tunable choices. This lab used one article and one chunking question, so the observed setting is not a universal optimum. Read the lesson.

THE ALMANAC

Chapter: Evaluating Retrieval-Augmented Generation.

RAG means retrieval-augmented generation: the system fetches passages and gives them to a model before it answers. Recall@k measures whether relevant passages appear in the first k results. Precision@k is the share of those k results marked relevant. MRR rewards finding the first relevant passage higher in the ranking; NDCG@k also uses graded relevance.

All data here are synthetic. The generation answers were hand-written; no LLM or API was run.
